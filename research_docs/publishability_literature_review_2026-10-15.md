# Is the v121 wheelchair trajectory result paper-worthy? Literature review

**Review date:** 2026-10-15  
**Scope:** Primary studies on wheelchair inertial kinematics/trajectory estimation, and adjacent inertial-navigation work that informs evaluation design. This is a focused scan, not a systematic review.

## Short answer

**Potentially paper-worthy as a focused methods/validation study, but the current v121 result is not yet strong enough to support a standalone generalizable-accuracy claim.** The topic is active and a real gap remains between wheelchair IMU-based speed/kinematics metrics and long-duration 2D trajectory reconstruction. The project has a concrete, reproducible candidate method and a meaningful frozen comparison: v121 reduces equal-person XY ATE RMSE from 100.702 mm (v34) to 99.304 mm on 71 records from four validation participants. But the relative gain is small (1.39 mm, about 1.4%), the validation cohort has only four people and has been exposed, and v121 inherits a baseline model whose training provenance includes 266 historical records. A publishable claim needs an independent acquisition and evaluation, participant-level uncertainty, a fully stated baseline/training lineage, and stronger evidence that the gain is repeatable and practically meaningful.

The defensible framing today is **“a constrained temporal residual correction for wheelchair IMU dead reckoning, with a small improvement in a previously exposed four-participant validation cohort; feasibility evidence only.”** Avoid “state of the art,” “generalizes to new users,” and “validated for deployment.”

Adjacent inertial-navigation benchmarks reinforce the importance of subject-disjoint evaluation and releasing reproducible data and code. RoNIN reports over 42.7 hours from 100 subjects and reports ATE/RTE on seen and unseen-subject splits ([project/paper page](https://ronin.cs.sfu.ca/)); that pedestrian setup is not a direct wheelchair baseline because sensor placement and dynamics differ.

## What the project has now

The saved v121 specification describes a frozen v34 motion baseline plus a fixed 16-state random recurrent reservoir over 15 existing gravity/rotation features. A regularized readout estimates bounded forward-speed and yaw residuals; the corrections are integrated at native timesteps. Candidate fitting used equal-person/equal-trial trajectory and heading losses on 207 TRAIN records from 12 people, with participant-held folds. The frozen validation report compares v121 to v34 on 71 records, four people and 105,821 supported frames; the equal-person XY ATE RMSE is 99.303735 mm vs 100.702119 mm. v121 passes the existing guard set, but the validation records were previously exposed. See [v121 preregistration/method](reservoir_path_v121.md), [v126 frozen comparison](frozen_validation_results_v126.md), and [current acquisition evidence status](acquisition-evidence-status.md).

This is a reasonable exploratory method contribution, especially if the paper centers on longitudinal wheelchair trajectory reconstruction and reports the model as a residual atop a physics/model-based prior. The metric and baseline are concrete; the data volume at the record/frame level is substantial. However, frames and repeated recordings do not replace independent participants: for generalization, the effective validation sample is four people, not 105,821 independent examples. The headline difference is also small, and the main benchmark is exposed. A single small-cohort bootstrap cannot repair those design limits.

## What must be added before a credible submission

1. **Acquire a new, prospectively frozen final cohort.** Keep model, preprocessing, clocks, synchronization, support masks, primary metric and guard rules locked before opening its labels. Include enough independent wheelchair users to estimate between-person uncertainty; choose the number with a preregistered power/precision rationale rather than treating clips or frames as independent samples.
2. **Resolve the lineage and independence of v34.** Document exactly which participants and records were used to fit the parent model and every preprocessing/calibration component. The current checkpoint explicitly says the parent model inherits 266 historical training records. Do not claim unseen-person generalization unless the entire pipeline—including its parent—is independent of final participants.
3. **Use a strong, fair comparator set.** Compare with frozen v34 and appropriate conventional wheelchair odometry/EKF baselines under identical inputs, support, initial pose, timing and reference alignment; add ablations for the reservoir/residual correction and report per-person results. Do not use a baseline trained on more or differently exposed labels without making that difference explicit.
4. **Make the endpoint valid and clinically/operationally interpretable.** Obtain synchronized optical ground truth or another justified trajectory reference over meaningful routes, disclose reference uncertainty, include actual acquisition endpoints, and report full-route ATE plus distance-normalized/local RTE, heading, endpoint error, and failure/coverage rates. Give participant-level confidence intervals and repeatability where repeated sessions exist.
5. **Close instrumentation and reproducibility gaps.** The current checkpoint flags independent timing, firmware/runtime identity, geometry, and new final acquisition as unresolved. Record these at collection; release de-identified data, preprocessing, reference evaluation code, and frozen inference artifacts if ethics/licensing permit.

## Practical publication decision

- **Could submit now?** A workshop, short methods note, or preliminary feasibility report may be reasonable if it labels the evaluation exploratory and prominently discloses exposure, four validation participants, parent-model lineage, and the small effect. Acceptance is uncertain; the novelty is currently an incremental architecture adaptation rather than a demonstrated new theory.
- **Would I recommend a full journal/conference claim now?** No. First run the independent acquisition above. The paper becomes more compelling if the main result reproduces on new participants and routes, beats strong synchronized baselines with participant-level uncertainty, and shows a practically meaningful improvement or a useful robustness advantage.
- **Most defensible contribution to test:** “Can a low-dimensional, frozen recurrent residual correct accumulated speed/yaw error in wheelchair IMU dead reckoning without worsening any participant or maneuver?” Treat this as a hypothesis for a new study, not a conclusion already established by v121.

## Q1 target and readiness plan

The requested target is a **Q1 journal**, not merely any publication. Two plausible targets, based on the current scope and the 2024 Journal Citation Reports tables, are:

- **IEEE Transactions on Neural Systems and Rehabilitation Engineering (TNSRE)** — strongest fit if the paper emphasizes the engineering method, wheelchair motion measurement, and rehabilitation technology. IEEE describes its scope as rehabilitative/neural biomedical engineering, including human-performance measurement and software applications for rehabilitation engineering. The 2024 JCR table lists it as Q1 in both Biomedical Engineering and Rehabilitation. [IEEE scope](https://open.ieee.org/publishing-options/topical-journals/); [2024 JCR table](https://library.utem.edu.my/phocadownload/Research-Resources/JCR2024%20Updated%20July2025.pdf).
- **Journal of NeuroEngineering and Rehabilitation (JNER)** — strongest fit if the work is tied to a clear rehabilitation or mobility-measurement use case. Its scope explicitly spans biomedical engineering, physical medicine and rehabilitation, new methods, and clinical implications. The 2024 JCR table lists it as Q1 in Rehabilitation. [JNER aims and scope](https://link.springer.com/journal/12984/aims-and-scope); [2024 JCR table](https://uefiscdi.gov.ro/resource-865584-JCR_2024.iunie2025.pdf).

Quartiles are indexed by database, subject category, and reporting year; confirm the exact criterion required by the user's institution and recheck the target journal's current quartile before submission. A Q1 label is a venue-selection goal, not evidence that the result meets that journal's novelty or rigor threshold.

**Q1 readiness gate for this project:** do not submit the current v121 result as a generalizable method. First resolve the parent-model 266-record lineage; freeze all preprocessing, time alignment, support, pose initialization, metrics and analysis before collecting labels on a new participant cohort; obtain synchronized ground truth and disclose its uncertainty; benchmark against v34 plus conventional odometry/EKF and ablate the recurrent correction; size the participant sample for between-person precision; report person-level effects/intervals and failure/coverage; and release code/data/artifacts to the extent consent and licensing allow. The primary endpoint should remain a predeclared trajectory measure with identical support and transparent RTE/endpoint availability. Do not claim real-time operation while the inherited pipeline uses offline temporal context.

**Recommended route:** aim first at TNSRE if the new evidence establishes robust engineering performance; prefer JNER if the study also demonstrates why the measurement advances rehabilitation research or practice. The currently saved 1.39% validation gain on four previously exposed participants is insufficient by itself for either Q1 target. The immediate next research milestone is a design and feasibility check for the new independent acquisition, not further tuning against the exposed validation set.
